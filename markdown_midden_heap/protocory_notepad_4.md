Section 1

You look out across the length of Riverfront Park through heavy, half-lidded eyes, shoulders slouching in dazed submission to the cloying evening summer heat, and you slide to mossy seat and let your head rest back on the criss-cross of vines that claim cement and rust-flaked bronze alike of the forgotten sewer fixture that marks park’s end and, for what feels like hours of waiting, your rather boring new best friend. You begin to doze, the low growing buzz of one flickering lightning bug after the other in counterpoint with the burbles and glubs of French Creek.

Section 2

A distant rumble, thunder, maybe, halts seizing sleep as you crack an eye across the sky for storm, dusk’s softening violets and deep blues staring back dumbly as rumble continues to grow like a herd of galloping horses joined by bellowing rhythmic shouts a distant, familiar song. 

Is that the theme from Jurassic Park?

The melody cracks into excited yips and barking laugh as, at last, Koryphanes the Chaldean bursts through thick clouds of dust kicked up by scaly truck of a beast she rides bareback like she was born there, and the wild snorting bleats from horned beak of what is unmistakably a dinosaur is drowned out by a booming shout, “OI CHUD LOOK LOOK I FOUND MY PRESENT FOUR DAYS EARLY HER NAME IS BAD BITCH BERTHA AND WE’RE IN LOVE!” The charging beast’s pounding feet clears the open grass and plows into the black brambled treeline. Koryphanes grips tight bony frill and horns, “I’M GONNA MARRY THIS BITCH GO GRAB THE CHURCH RICE THAT MAKES PIGEONS EXPLOOOOODE!!!” 

Section 3

Your flailing crab crawl back is stopped short by  sheer sloping earthy wall, the wild amber eyes that rush to meet you with feral grin all you see as she twists the beast’s horn hard toward your left and leaps from its back as it crashes through bushes and splashes into creek, its churning feet fading into silence before your pounding heart has the chance.

Koryphanes looks perfectly pleased and at ease while she watches the last bits of beast recede into the lingering light, “Well, you certainly don’t see one of those every day, huh?” Her hands perch pert upon her hips with a soft shake of her head and a satisfied smile, “Wow. Tricyclotopalops.”
