◎.  Method

♈︎.  The virtues attributed to the seven classical planets are often a crucial part of the practice of many modern magicians.  Through the use of sympathetic correspondences that see each planet bound to things like plants and metals, the power of a planet can be most strongly channeled into a ritual to ensure its success.  Access to many of these materials, however, is no easy feat for the magician on a budget, so I thought I'd give us all a hand by sharing musical materials that can work just as well.

♎︎.  You'll find here a large list of the musical correspondences of each planet that can aid in practical magic.  Some of these, such as the vowels, pitches, and modes of the diatonic scale, have been taken straight from historical texts, while others were arrived at through processes of reason and intuition.  The character and aspect of each planet in various sources was used to determine which evocative musical qualities should be bound to each, and where it could not, the spirit of the planet was directly engaged for guidance.

♋︎.  I've taken great strides to offer something more than just the wanton association enjoyed by the likes of Liber 777 and other products of particular neopagan performers.  In addition to pouring through all of the relevant source material I could find and having a comprehensive understanding of music theory, I verified the appropriateness of each musical item through means of divination.  After, each correspondence was clearly consecrated during the hour of the planet through invocation and repeated performance.

♑︎.  First, we'll explore some of the magical and musical sources that gave birth to these correspondences.  Then, each planet will be reviewed in turn, with a list of the musical virtues of each culminating in audio examples. Finally, we'll consider some of the many means with which to put all of this to productive practice.  Some of what I've presented here may be perplexing to the untrained musician, but fear not, as all will be eventually clarified in upcoming writings regarding a personally developed esoteric music theory.  Regardless, I believe at least some of this will be immediately useful for everyone, layman and musician alike.

⁂.  Sources

♌︎.  Like much of the modern occult, the character of the planets can be traced back to the literal characters celebrated in ancient myths.  These gods, perhaps originating from a common source such as the speakers of Proto-Indo-European, Proto-Afroasiatic, or some combination of them and more, retained many of their attributes and narrative actions across cultures.  The Roman Venus, for example, bore a compelling resemblance to the Greek Ἀφροδίτη (Aphrodite), the Babylonian 𒀭𒈹 (Inanna), and the Vedic शुक्र (Shukra), and this ubiquitous distinction of each over time and space should assure us that these characters are not to be taken lightly when understanding the planets.

♒︎.  Of the many esoteric texts I studied for this endeavor, I found the Tetrabiblos, the Picatrix, and the Key of Solomon to be particularly helpful.  While the Tetrabiblos, a 2nd century CE work in Ancient Greek by Ptolemy that has served as a lasting authority on the nature of the heavens, acts as a stellar example of an astrological consideration of the planets, the Key of Solomon, a 14th or 15th century CE grimoire pseudoepigraphically attributed to the biblical King Solomon, wonderfully captures the rituals associated with invoking the planetary powers.  The Picatrix, written sometime in the 10th or 11th centuries CE by an unknown author, neatly blends the two in both chronology and focus.

♏︎.  Three sources from musical antiquity were also used for three particular correspondences.  Nicomachus, an Ancient Greek music theorist and mathematician, supplied the basis for the correspondence of pitch with each planet.  Marcus, the founder of the Marcosian sect of Gnosticism, was used for the vowels associated with each planet.  The earlier image of the classical spheres musically connected with the muses and planets was found in a work by the 15th century CE music theorist and composer Franchinus Gaffurius.  By observing the upwards series of tonus and semitonium between each of the planets on the right, we can conclude that each planet is associated with one of what modern western music calls the seven modes of the diatonic scale.

♉︎.  There are some concepts of music that will be helpful to share, albeit with but the bewildering brevity our scope here affords. 

An interval is the distance between two pitches.  There are twelve simple intervals, which are all contained within an octave, something that can be understood as the distance between a C on a keyboard and the C above it.  A series of intervals is called a scale, and the notes that are members of that scale are called its degrees.  When a degree of a scale is chosen to act as the tonic, or the central pitch around which all others revolve, one is selecting one of the scale's modes to use.  As the diatonic scale has seven degrees, it contains seven modes.  Modes can be notated using numbers that represent the intervals between degrees inside of parenthesis.  For example, (2,2,1,2,2,2,1) represents the Ionian mode, and if I were to have a tonic of D, this would result in (D, E, F#, G, A, B, C#).

Groups of pitches are called pitch sets.  The pitches of a pitch set can be played all at once (vertical or harmonic) or in a series (horizontal or melodic) and are written using characters inside of square brackets.  If we look at the pitch set [C,E,G] alongside the pitch set [G,B,D], we'll see that their members share a relationship of intervals.  This relationship is called a pitch set class, and it would be written as [0,4,7], where 0 represents any starting pitch, 4 represents a pitch 4 above that starting pitch, and 7 represents a pitch 7 above the starting pitch.

Though intimately connected, meter and rhythm are different things.  A meter is composed of equally lengthed beats with certain patterns of stress that provide the pulse of a piece.  Meters are referenced using a time signature, which is written as a fraction but bears a different meaning, where the top number is the number of beats per measure and the bottom number is the type of beat, where 2 is a half note, 4 is a quarter note, 8 is an eighth note, etc.  4/4 would have a measure composed of four quarter notes.  A rhythm is composed of relative durations of stressed beats that may differ from those of the meter it occurs in.  A rhythm can be written using the durations as ratios inside of curly brackets.  For example, {3:3:2} would be a rhythm counted as "ONE two three ONE two three ONE two."

⨁.  Practice

♐︎.  There are a myriad of ways to employ these musical correspondences when working with planetary magic.  In essence, all one must do is have one or more of these musical items physically occur during the spell, whether through headphones, a speaker, or an instrument.  Putting them to use need not be complicated, and I've identified three broad types of use based on a practitioner's musical abilities.  Let's look at the nature of each and the ways they can employ these magical correspondences during ritual activity.

♊︎.  The first type is perceptive, which involves simply experiencing the musical virtue from an outside source and is meant for those not yet musically inclined.  Any music that features any of the planet's virtues can be listened to in order to channel its strength, and the more, the better.  Let it resonate within you before your ritual, attuning yourself to the planet's nature and solidifying your connection.  If possible, continue playing it as you perform the spell, though some music is obviously less suitable than others when attention must be paid and words spoken.

♓︎.  The second type is performative, which involves expressing the music using an instrument and is meant for those with moderate musical abilities.  This can be done by playing something prewritten, improvising using one of the virtues, or even just somehow expressing a virtue once.  Whether by using an instrument, voice, or simply drumming on a conducive surface, performing puts you in the role of the planet, casting yourself as a sympathetic correspondence in your own right to strengthen the ritual. 

♍︎.  The third type is poetic, which involves creating a new piece of music and is meant for those with the ability to compose.  The intention associated with the act of composition and the nature of the ritual being performed brilliantly charges the space with planetary power and produces a piece that can be used over and over again for similar effect.  While the most efficacious, it is certainly the most difficult and out of the grasp of many folks' busy lives.  Luckily, I've devoted quite a bit of time to refining this art and I'm always happy to help.  Be sure to check Ritual Reflections for new planetary works or even to request a custom piece for a particular spell!
