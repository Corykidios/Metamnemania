🜃.  Information

♑︎. We're all familiar with the twelve signs of the zodiac, but what all might they really be?  From the Ancient Greek ζῳδιακός κύκλος (zodiakos kuklos), the word literally means "a circle of little living beings."  It seems to have antiquitous origins in constellations that divide the heavens into twelve equal parts.  Unphased by a jagged line we seem comfortable pretending resembles a ram, contemporary culture seems almost exclusively interested in the zodiac as a means of understanding personality, only occasionally acknowledging with jet black pupils hidden under garlands of flowers things like astrological ages and the procession of equinoxes.  Might the zodiac be used for anything other than star-watching, navel-gazing, and producing pop hits of yesteryear?  I believe so, but understanding how demands you bear with a bit of a detour.  Put on your comfiest sneakers and follow me to the nature of systems and symbols.

♉︎.  The word "system", derived from the Ancient Greek σύστημα (systema) meaning "things placed together," is used by many people in many ways, though few have even fancied reflecting on it.  Don't worry, I already did.  A system is a whole composed of sets of related parts, properties, and processes that fulfill functions.  What, then, isn't a system?  Probably nothing.  A sentence is a system that expresses a thought.  A recipe is a system that produces a meal.  A body, a community, nature, the universe itself, all these are systems, overlapping and interlocking.  Sadly, systems made by people can be ugly without three things.  The first is an ontology, or an identification and naming of all parts, properties, and processes.  The second is a taxonomy, or an arrangement of related things into sets that are organized into categories.  The third is a methodology, or an account of how a system fulfills its functions.

♍︎.  Humans seem made to make meaning.  We careen through our environment, relying on signs to guide our course.  The meanings we give to them are almost always functional and range from the definition of a word to a feeling of love for a person or body pillow.  The combination of a sign and a meaning is called a symbol, which you may be surprised to find also originates from Ancient Greek, here σύμβολον (symbolon), which literally means "two things thrown together."  Memory is the means with which we store, relate, and recall the symbols we encounter most.  Powerful though a mind may be, like the durability of a body pillow, it has its limits.  As only so many symbols can be actively considered at once, the organization of relations of systems help us compartmentally manage a multitude of symbols in a way otherwise almost impossible.

🜄. Simple Parts

♋︎.  The parts, properties, processes, sets, and relations of a system are referred to as its elements, which my enamored heart must recall has a counterpart in the Ancient Greek στοιχεῖον (stoicheion), a word that initially meant one of a row but came to mean any irreducible component of something else.  This and the English word “element” both also refer to the four classical elements of fire, air, gracefully flowing water, and earth.  The twelve signs of the zodiac are largely composed of a single element and one of three modalities, being cardinal or first, fixed or middle, and mutable or last.  The following table of glyphs and names should soothe any confusion:

♏︎.  Stop.  Grab a pen and paper.  Write these nineteen glyphs and names by hand, these inklings of a new alphabet.  Do so until this first set of esoteric tools is safely stored deep in memory.  Then we may move on to how the four powers can form the four elements.  The four powers are dry, hot, wet, and cold.  Dry is opposite of wet, hot of cold.  Fire is hot and dry, air wet and hot, water cold and wet, and earth dry and cold:

![](https://images.squarespace-cdn.com/content/v1/61c9d87b0d34d2002773a81c/1641279796121-HM00L8VRWTFRD1H265Q8/table2.png)

Thus, fire is similar to air and earth but different from water, air is similar to fire and water but different from earth, water is similar to air and earth but different from fire, and earth is similar to water and fire but different from air.  Learn this well.

♓︎.  Aries, the ram, has properties of cardinal and fire, hot and dry.  Taurus, the bull, has properties of fixed and earth, dry and cold.  Gemini, the twins, has properties of mutable and air, wet and hot.  Cancer, the crab, has properties of cardinal and water, cold and wet.  Leo, the lion, has properties of fixed and fire, hot and dry.  Virgo, the virgin, has properties of mutable and earth, dry and cold.  Libra, the scales, has properties of cardinal and air, wet and hot.  Scorpio, the scorpion, has properties of fixed and water, cold and wet.  Sagittarius, the archer, has properties of mutable and fire, hot and dry.  Capricorn, the sea-goat, has properties of cardinal and earth, dry and cold.  Aquarius, the water-bearer, has properties of fixed and air, wet and hot.  Pisces, the love-drunk fishes of faltering faith, has properties of mutable and water, cold and wet.

🜁.  Compound Parts

♎︎.  Along with the one that is the whole, the powers, elements, modalities, and zodiac form a system of twenty-four related parts, each a symbol with a glyph and name as a sign and a property as a meaning.  Their true power begins to be gleaned when the meanings are cast aside and the signs all that remain.  Without meanings, the system becomes nothing more than a network of related parts.  As a tipping scale is no fun for anyone, new meanings must be made for the signs that still retains their relationships, the system's meanings now cast in some unique and novel context of your own design.  Imagine that you're making something with twelve parts, each bound to a zodiac sign.  You give wet the property of sincere, hot the property of intense, which makes air sincere and intense.  You give aggressive to cardinal, and thus Libra, being cardinal and air, must be sincere, intense, and aggressive.

♒︎.  With the sign of the one or the whole now meaning the entirety of a work, give the signs of the elements and modalities as many meanings as your work requires, so long as they're built to blend together.  Remember, each zodiac sign is a unique pairing of one element and one modality.  Sometimes elements may be formed from the four powers, and others not.  If the powers are deemed useful, bear close in mind their pattern.  The property of hot must be the opposite of cold, that of dry the opposite of wet.  The property of hot and of cold must be able to blend with that of dry and wet, and vice versa.  See how the four powers and three modes or the four elements and three modes are mixed to make an interwoven twelve, a one of many, a whole of parts.  See how you, the artist, are a god and your artwork a cosmos.

♊︎.  Hm, too abstract?  I'll help.  First, give meaning to the whole, a name and nature for the artwork to be.  Next, decide if you want to define the elements alone or by using the powers.  If using the powers, create meanings for each of them to produce a related four elements with two meanings each.  If not, simply create meanings for the elements.  Next, create meanings for the three modalities.  Ensure that the modalities and elements are made to fit together and that their meanings reflect the needs and goals of the artwork.  Have fun and experiment!  Let fate help weave your web!  Finally, with meaningful properties bound and balanced to the one, the elements, and the modalities, you can begin creating the actual parts of the work that the zodiac represent!  The forest can be forgotten in favor of focusing on only one of twelve trees at a time, bringing them to life with naught but the two or three properties of each in mind.

🜂: Application

♈︎.  Let's put all this together before patience wears too thin!  The system of the zodiac is great because an unfathomable set of twelve can be easily managed in the mind in groups of three or four.  The twenty-four parts use an easily written glyph and a colorful name to help create symbols to store in memory even when those symbols have no meaning.  A work of art is simply information expressed through a communicable medium and thus a system.  Creating seven related things that produce twelve is easier than creating twelve unique things.  Thinking of twelve things in groups of three and four is the only way the human mind can come close to conceiving them as a whole.  All things are systems, including a work of art.  All systems have parts.  Twelve is a good number of parts.  Let's help those who disagree limp along with a little example.

♌︎.  You want to write a blog about using the zodiac to make art.  You define the one as "A Beginner's Guide to Making Art with the Zodiac."  You decide the zodiac will be paragraphs arranged into four elemental sections, with earth first, then water, air, and fire.  You want to build the elements from the powers and decide to bind meanings associated with subjects to them.  You know the second half will be more complicated than the first half, so you give the meaning of "basic" to cold and "advanced" to hot.  You reason that the second and third elemental sections will most likely focus on the inner mechanics of the system, while the first and fourth will provide outside context.  Thinking yourself clever, you give the meaning of "outer" to dry and "inner" to wet.  The first element, earth, is thus basic outer and named "Information," while water, basic inner, is named "Simple Parts," air, advanced inner, is named "Compound Parts," and fire, advanced outer, is named "Application."  To help each section flow, you give to cardinal "introduction," to fixed "exposition," and to mutable "conclusion."  To add some fun, you decide to associate additional meanings of style with the powers and modalities, with dry as "sarcastic," hot "intense," wet "sincere," cold "subdued," cardinal "aggressive," fixed "mysterious," and mutable "playful."  You give a name to each paragraph, which produces the following:

♐︎.  Neat, no?  Regardless of medium, whether visual art, informative prose, narrative prose, dance, drama, poetry, music, sculpture, world-building, or even just general systems, this system can work wonders in ways that I'll elaborate on in a future blog.  And this is just a basic version!  The zodiac can be furthered defined with parts like the poles of light and dark and the four seasons, or each expanded upon into groups of three called decans or groups of thirty called degrees, and that's only some of what's in store.  For now, I challenge you to create something made of twelve parts using these twenty-four, whether as intricate as a work of art or as simple as a meal or a to-do list.  Whatever it is, share your creations and considerations in the comments below!  Until we meet again, happy hunting.
